Cursive Kitchen

Privacy Policy

Effective date: October 11, 2026

This policy explains how the Cursive Kitchen app for iPhone, and its Save to Cursive Kitchen share extension, handle information. Cursive Kitchen is made by an independent developer (“we”, “us”). See also the Terms of Use.

The short version

  • We do not collect any personal data. There is no account and no server of ours. Your recipes, photos and grocery list are stored only on your iPhone and are never sent to us.
  • No ads, no analytics, no tracking and no third-party SDKs.
  • The app goes online only to import a recipe from a web page when you ask (it contacts that website directly), and to talk to Apple's App Store about the optional Cursive Kitchen Pro subscription.
  • Scanning (text recognition), language detection and translation use Apple's built-in technology on your iPhone.
  • The camera, the photos you pick, the clipboard and notifications are used only when you choose a feature that needs them.

1. What is stored on your iPhone

Cursive Kitchen stores your recipes in its own private storage on your iPhone. Other apps cannot read it, and it is not synced to iCloud or any server.

  • Recipes: title, description, servings and yield, prep and cook times, ingredients, steps, tags, favorites, the source link, site name and author, your own source note (for example, “Grandma's recipe box”), and whether you marked the recipe as your own or as shared with permission.
  • The original: how a recipe came in (scan, paste, web or typed), the original text, the original web description, the detected language and the original scan photo. These are kept unchanged so you can always go back to them, and they are deleted with the recipe.
  • Translations: the language a recipe was translated from, and when.
  • Photos: the original photo of a scanned recipe card or cookbook page, and up to 10 photos per recipe that you add in the editor. The app saves a new, resized copy of each (up to 2048 pixels on the longest side, as a JPEG). It never saves anything to your Photos library.
  • Collections, cook history and grocery list: your collections; the date of each time you tap I Cooked This; and grocery items with quantities, whether they are checked off, and which recipes they came from.
  • Cooking timers: label, duration, end time and status. Finished timers are removed automatically about 6 hours after they end.
  • Import records: which items from your share inbox were saved as recipes, and when.
  • Free-use counter: how many free scans and translations have been used on this device.
  • Preferences: how units are shown (original, US or metric) and whether the app has already asked you to rate it.
  • Housekeeping for safe restores: a random ID for your data set, and during a restore, working copies of your data and a small status file. If a restored file fails the app's checks, the app keeps that copy aside in its private storage. It is not deleted automatically and is removed when you delete the app.

Share inbox

When you use Save to Cursive Kitchen from another app's share sheet, the shared link or text (up to 200,000 characters) and the time are saved in a folder on your iPhone that only Cursive Kitchen and its share extension can use. The item stays in the app's Inbox until you import it or remove it. The share extension does not connect to the internet.

Subscription record

After the app checks your Cursive Kitchen Pro status with the App Store, it stores a small record in the iOS Keychain on this iPhone: the transaction ID, the expiration date, the renewal status, the end of any grace period, and when it was last checked. It contains no name, email address or payment details. It is set to stay on this device only and is never sent to us.

Backup files

When you tap Create Backup File, the app creates a file with every recipe (including the originals and cook history), your collections, your grocery list, your import records and all photos, plus the app's identifier and version and the time of export. Running timers and the free-use counter are not included. The file is not encrypted. It is created in the app's temporary storage. The app does not delete it itself, but iOS may clear it, and it is removed when you delete the app. It leaves the app only if you save or send it somewhere using the share sheet.

Timer alerts

For each running timer, iOS holds a scheduled alert on your iPhone with the title “Timer done” and the timer's label (for example, “Simmer · 10–12 min”) until it goes off or you stop the timer.

Things kept only while you use them

  • Unsaved edits, including a scanned photo, are kept in memory until you tap Save.
  • Clipboard: the app reads the clipboard only when you tap Paste Link or Paste from Clipboard, and iOS may ask your permission first. To decide whether to show those buttons, the app only checks whether the clipboard holds a link or text, without reading it.

2. What leaves your iPhone

Nothing is sent to us. Cursive Kitchen has no account and no server of ours. The app itself connects to the internet in only two cases: importing a recipe from a web page when you ask it to, and talking to Apple's App Store about the subscription.

Importing a recipe from a website

When you tap Import Recipe, the app downloads that one web page directly from the website, not through any server of ours, and pulls out the recipe information.

  • It happens only when you start it. Opening an item from your share inbox only fills in the address.
  • Only secure (https) addresses are used, including for any redirects.
  • The app sends no cookies, stores no cookies and keeps no web cache.
  • The website, and any service it redirects to or uses to deliver the page, receives what any website receives: your IP address and the standard request details iOS adds (such as a user-agent string with the app's name and the system version).
  • Only that one page is downloaded (up to 5 MB). Pictures linked from the page are not downloaded.
  • We never receive the address, the page or your IP address.

The website's own privacy policy applies to that visit.

The App Store and Cursive Kitchen Pro

The app uses Apple's StoreKit to show the subscription price, let you subscribe, restore purchases and manage your subscription, and check whether your subscription is active (when the app opens or returns to the screen, when your connection comes back, and after a purchase or restore). Apple handles payment and your Apple Account on its own purchase screens. The app uses the results only on your iPhone. We have no server that receives or checks your purchases. Apple gives developers sales and subscription reports that do not include your name, email address or payment details. Apple's handling of your information is described in the Apple Privacy Policy. Subscription terms are in the Terms of Use.

Rating prompt

Once, after you have saved at least three recipes, the app may ask Apple to show its standard rating prompt. Any rating or review you give goes to Apple. The app is not told what you chose.

Translation

When you tap Translate, the recipe's title, description, ingredients, steps and headings are passed to Apple's Translation feature, which translates on your iPhone. If the language is not installed yet, iOS asks you before downloading it from Apple. The app does not send your recipe text to any server.

Scanning, language detection and connection status

Text in scanned photos is read with Apple's Vision framework on your iPhone, and the recipe's language is detected on your iPhone. The app also watches whether your iPhone is online, which sends nothing.

Sharing

With the share sheet you can send a recipe as text, your grocery list, or a backup file to Messages, Mail, AirDrop, Files, iCloud Drive or another app. A shared recipe includes its title, ingredients and source or link; the full text is included only if you turn that on, and never for recipes imported from websites. Whatever you share goes only to the place you choose. We do not receive it. Backup files are not encrypted, so keep them somewhere private.

Links that open outside the app

Tapping a recipe's source link opens it in your web browser, and that website receives your browser's usual request. Privacy Policy and Terms of Use open pages hosted on GitHub Pages, so GitHub receives the information any website receives, such as your IP address and browser type (see the GitHub Privacy Statement). Open Settings buttons open iOS Settings on your iPhone.

Support email

The app shows our support address as text. The app does not send email. If you write to us from your own email app, we receive your email address and whatever you include, and we use it only to answer you. We keep these emails only as long as we need them to help you, and we delete them within 12 months after your question is resolved, or sooner if you ask. Our email is provided by Google (Gmail).

iPhone backups

If you back up your iPhone to iCloud or to a computer, iOS includes Cursive Kitchen's data (recipes, photos, the share inbox and preferences) in that backup, as it does for most apps. The subscription record in the Keychain is set to this device only and does not move to a new iPhone. You and Apple control these backups. We have no access to them.

Crash reports shared by Apple

If you have chosen in iOS to share analytics with app developers (Settings > Privacy & Security > Analytics & Improvements), Apple may give us crash reports and usage statistics that do not identify you. You can turn this off at any time. The app itself contains no crash-reporting or analytics code.

3. Permissions

  • Camera, only when you tap Take Photo to scan a handwritten recipe card or cookbook page. The photo is read on your iPhone and kept as the recipe's original. It is not saved to your Photos library. If camera access is off, the app tells you and offers a button to open iOS Settings.
  • Notifications, asked when you start your first cooking timer, and used only for “Timer done” alerts. If you say no, timers ring only while the app is open, and the app tells you so.
  • Photos: the app has no access to your photo library. Choose Photo (for scanning) and Add Photos (in the editor) use Apple's photo picker, which gives the app only the photos you select.
  • Clipboard: read only when you tap a paste button, as described above.
  • Files: the system file picker gives the app only the backup file you select to restore.
  • Translation languages: iOS asks before downloading a language.
  • Not used: location, contacts, microphone, speech recognition, calendars and reminders, health, motion, Bluetooth, local network, Face ID and tracking.

4. Ads, analytics and third-party code

  • No ads, analytics, crash-reporting or tracking tools.
  • The app does not use the advertising identifier or any other identifier to recognize you or your device.
  • No third-party code or SDKs. The app and its share extension use only Apple's system frameworks and our own code.
  • We do not sell or share personal information.

5. Deleting your information

There is no account and no server, so we hold nothing to delete. Everything is on your iPhone, and you delete it there:

  • Delete a recipe (you will be asked to confirm). This removes its ingredients, steps, photos, original text and photo, and cook history, and stops its timers and their alerts. Grocery items you added from it stay on your grocery list. Individual cook-history entries cannot be removed on their own; they go when the recipe is deleted.
  • Remove photos in the editor. The photo file is deleted after you tap Save.
  • Delete a collection by swiping it. The recipes in it are kept.
  • Grocery list: adding and removing items (by swiping, Remove Checked Items or Clear List) requires an active Cursive Kitchen Pro subscription. Without Pro, an existing list can still be viewed, checked off and shared, but items can't be removed, so the list is removed only by restoring a backup or deleting the app.
  • Timers: stop or dismiss them. Finished timers are removed automatically about 6 hours after they end.
  • Share inbox: swipe an item and tap Remove. An item is also removed after you import it and save the recipe.
  • Restore from Backup File replaces all recipe data after you confirm. After the restored data passes the app's checks, the old data is deleted.
  • Everything: delete the app. iOS removes everything the app stored, including the free-use counter and preferences, which have no reset inside the app.

One exception: iOS does not always remove Keychain items when an app is deleted, so the subscription record (transaction ID, dates and renewal status only) may remain on the iPhone after you delete the app.

Backup files and recipes you saved or sent elsewhere, and your iCloud or computer backups of your iPhone, are outside the app. Delete them where you keep them. To stop paying for Pro, cancel the subscription with Apple (see the Terms of Use); deleting the app does not cancel it.

6. Children

Cursive Kitchen is not directed at children under 13 and is not part of the App Store's Kids category. The app does not collect personal information from anyone, of any age, so we do not knowingly collect personal information from children. Photos and recipes stay on the iPhone unless the user chooses to share them.

If you believe a child has sent us personal information by email, contact us and we will delete it.

7. Your choices and rights

Because the app sends us nothing, we hold no personal data from it, so there is nothing for us to give you access to, correct, export or delete. You can export all of your own recipes at any time with Create Backup File. If you have emailed us, you can ask us to delete your messages at any time.
